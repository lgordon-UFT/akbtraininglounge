“Thanksgiving is three seatings and 220 covers. Can AI actually help me build the staffing plan, or is that still a human job?”

✅ Top Answer

It's genuinely good at this, but only if you give it the constraints. Load the Operations Manager persona, then list: coverage minimum per seating, who's already requested off, holiday pay rules, and the fact that we want strong floor coverage this season because the 15% goal rides on service quality.

Ask for a table by seating with a short list of gaps underneath. It'll catch conflicts you'd miss at 11pm. You still make the call — but you're editing a draft instead of starting cold.

— Operations Manager persona · see Session 3's worked example

“What's the best way to use AI to answer a tough Google review without sounding defensive or corporate? We have 3,000+ reviews and the generic replies read badly.”

✅ Top Answer

The fix is a persona, not a better prompt. Before you ask for the reply, paste the Marketing Lead persona — it already carries our voice rules, including the words we never use. Then give it the actual review text.

Two things that make the difference: name the specific thing that went wrong rather than apologizing in general, and never close with an invitation to "discuss offline." That reads as damage control. Thank them, own the specific miss, say what changes, and leave the door open warmly.

— Marketing Lead persona + Session 2 workflow

“Can I keep our chart of accounts in a Claude Project so I stop re-pasting it every close? It's the same setup every single time.”

✅ Top Answer

Yes — this is exactly what Projects are for. In Claude, click Projects in the left sidebar, create one called "AKB Finance," and paste the Finance Analyst persona into the project instructions. Every new chat inside that project loads it automatically.

You can also upload files to the project — last year's P&L, your account mapping, a blank variance template. Claude can reference them directly instead of you pasting figures each time. One caution: keep anything with bank or card detail out of it.

— Session 2 resource page

“How do I build a prompt that writes wine pairing notes for our list? When I ask plainly, it suggests wines we don't even carry.”

✅ Top Answer

That's a Context problem — element two from Session 1. The AI has no idea what's on our list, so it invents plausible wines. Paste the actual by-the-glass list into the prompt, then ask it to pair only from that list. Add the constraint explicitly: "Do not suggest any wine not on this list."

Then add Format: ask for one sentence a server can say at the table, not a paragraph of tasting notes. Nobody recites three sentences about minerality on a Saturday night.

— Service Mentor persona

“Is it safe to paste real work documents into ChatGPT? I'm not sure where the line is.”

✅ Top Answer

Treat it like email to someone outside the restaurant. Fine to paste: draft schedules without surnames, menu text, public policy, general templates, vendor quotes. Not fine: guest payment details, anyone's personal contact info, medical or disciplinary matters, anything from a personnel file.

When in doubt, anonymize first — "Server A," "Line Cook B." The AI almost never needs the real name to give you a useful answer.

— Operations Manager persona
